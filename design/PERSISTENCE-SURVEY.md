# The Persistence Frontier: A Survey of Why Complexity Might Stick

## The problem, as measured

Primordial Soup keeps finding the same story. Branching structure ignites in every world tested, and the Bolder Mutations follow up found it surviving almost nowhere: only two of six seeds held depth two or more through the final fifteen continuous minutes of a forty five minute run, both behind an eye lineage that held first. The crown run, seed 60606 to one hundred eighty minutes, is the clearest single case: a complete animal, three parts deep and mostly eyed, held from roughly minute ninety four through minute one hundred forty four, a fifty minute golden age, then lost its eyes entirely by minute one hundred sixty two and reverted to near featureless bodies by minute one hundred eighty, despite two hundred sixty six generations of unbroken breeding. Six fresh ninety minute seeds replicated the absence more than the presence: zero reproduced a sustained complete animal.

Geography is the one lever that has moved this number. The incubator trials found the archipelago holding structure to minute one hundred twenty in four worlds out of four, one of them, seed 9002, carrying a fully eyed population continuously for the final sixty six minutes, sustained by local extinction and migration driven recolonization rather than by avoiding it. A bigger, safer ocean scenario bought nothing. Refugia geometry has a sharp edge: loose straits produce stable, growing, and totally sterile populations, not one eyed creature fixed across the sweep, while tighter straits raise the churn that keeps rescuing structure. Geography shelters complexity; nothing yet makes it permanent. That gap is the persistence frontier: not whether complexity can appear, which is settled, but what would make it a resting state instead of a peak.

Three candidate levers are on the table: necessity, a resource only complex bodies can reach; the peacock ratchet, mate choice that rewards ornament; and entanglement, developmental lock in that makes old organs expensive to shed. These three, and the hard requirements they must clear, come from design/DESIGN-PERSISTENCE.md, written before this survey so its four open questions could not be bent to fit an answer already in mind.

## The literature

**Digital evolution and long run artificial worlds.** Lenski's long term evolution experiment with E. coli, running since 1988, is the closest real precedent here: a citrate using lineage took roughly thirty one thousand generations to appear, built on earlier mutations nobody could see in advance (Blount, Borland, and Lenski, 2008, PNAS). Avida, the digital arm of the same program, showed complex logic functions evolving by building on simpler functions already favored on their own (Lenski, Ofria, Pennock, and Adami, 2003, Nature). Ray's Tierra, self replicating code with no fitness function written in (1991), and Holland's Echo (1995) set the same template this project follows: give agents a real economy and let meaning emerge.

**Open endedness: definitions and measures.** Bedau, Snyder, and Packard gave the field its cleanest yardstick, evolutionary activity that is absent, bounded, or unbounded, three classes (1998). A world that ignites structure and prunes it back, what every classic dish here does, is textbook class two; the persistence frontier is asking what turns class two into class three. Bedau and a long coauthor list later named open endedness a grand challenge outright (2000), and the OEE workshop at York found the field still struggles to separate its hallmarks from the mechanisms that explain them (Taylor, Bedau, and colleagues, 2016). Lehman and Stanley's novelty search (2011) and Brant and Stanley's minimal criterion coevolution (2017) both found that rewarding difference, or mere survival, beat optimizing for an explicit objective, worth noting since this project has never optimized for complexity either, only priced it. Soros and Stanley's Chromaria (2014) tried to name necessary conditions for open endedness directly.

**Sexual selection models.** Fisher's runaway process (1930) is the founding idea: a mate preference and the trait it prefers can become genetically linked and accelerate each other past what natural selection alone would allow. Lande (1981) and Kirkpatrick (1982) turned the argument into population genetic models and confirmed it as a real, stable line of equilibria; Lande's version also produces speciation by sexual isolation, notable given this project's own hue based speciation results. Zahavi's handicap principle (1975) offers a competing logic: an ornament is favored because it is costly, so cost itself becomes the honest signal.

**Ratchets, neutral construction, and one way doors.** Muller described a ratchet by which small asexual populations accumulate deleterious mutations, since the least loaded class can be lost by chance and never recovered (1964); Felsenstein named it Muller's ratchet in the paper that made recombination's advantage rigorous (1974). Primordial Soup breeds sexually with crossover, so this exact ratchet does not apply directly. The mechanism that does apply is constructive neutral evolution: Stoltzfus (1999) and, in a widely read follow up, Gray, Lukes, Archibald, Keeling, and Doolittle (2010, Science) showed how a part can become required not because it was selected for the dependency but because dependency accumulates for free once other parts rely on it, and reversal grows unlikely once several parts depend on each other at once. Riedl called that accumulating cost burden (1978, from his 1975 German original); Wimsatt named the same idea generative entrenchment, traits many later steps depend on become conservative by construction (1986), this survey's own one way doors in plain language. Maynard Smith and Szathmary's major transitions (1995) show the same lock in at biology's largest scale.

**Niche construction and metapopulation rescue.** Odling Smee, Laland, and Feldman argued organisms routinely modify their own selective environment and then must live inside what they built (2003), close to the shape of this project's reef mechanic. Levins gave the field its metapopulation model, extinction and recolonization across patches (1969); Brown and Kodric Brown added the rescue effect, immigration lowering local extinction risk directly (1977, Ecology), close to what the incubator trials already found in the archipelago.

**Resource gated complexity and escalation.** Vermeij's escalation hypothesis (1987) argues that predators and prey ratchet up defensive and offensive morphology against each other over geological time. This is the least tightly matched citation here and should be flagged: Vermeij describes millions of years of shell thickening, not a ninety minute dish, and no citation states cleanly that a morphology gated resource will keep a population complex rather than just once select for it. McShea and Brandon's Zero Force Evolutionary Law (2010) cautions that complexity rises by default, absent constraint, with no selection pushing it, so a lever's real job may be removing what already taxes complexity down, namely the metabolic rent this project prices on every structure.

A standing caveat covers this whole survey, echoing the project's own honest limitations section: almost everything above was measured at population sizes, timescales, or abstraction levels far from a dish holding dozens of creatures for ninety minutes, and none of it was tested on a system with hue gated mating and priced metabolism. Every mechanism below is a real, citable idea; whether it works at this project's own speed and scale is what this survey cannot answer from the library alone.

## Necessity: food only complex bodies can reach

**What the literature predicts.** Escalation and general ecomorphology predict that when access to a resource is gated by form, form should track that gate for as long as the resource is worth contesting. The Zero Force caution suggests the gate mostly needs to stop taxing complexity back down, not actively reward it, since a default tendency toward complexity may already be present.

**A cheap, deterministic mechanism.** Primordial Soup already ships the closest thing to this: the reef's algae bonus at grazed equilibrium. Necessity would gate a food source explicitly behind a body property already in the genome, reef top algae reachable only by a part with enough reach, or algae visible only within an eye's cone. Both are placement and gating checks on existing fields, no new random stream required, evaluated at the same growth ticks that already cache body state.

**How to measure it.** Exactly the near versus control protocol already used for the reef garden: seeded runs, multiple seeds, the standard complexity measures of branch depth, eyed share, and paddler fixation, compared between creatures holding the gated niche and those without it, watched across the crown run's full window rather than a single snapshot.

**Risks.** The project's own reef replication is the sharpest warning available: a six fold garden effect in one seed averaged below parity across five fresh seeds, saturation in good times masking it entirely. A gate calibrated too weak will be invisible the same way; too strong, it can starve everyone lacking the trait, and with the founder bottleneck already costing every world roughly sixty percent of its founders in the first two minutes, that risks compounding an existing fragility.

## The peacock ratchet: mate choice for elaborate bodies

**What the literature predicts.** Fisher, Lande, and Kirkpatrick predict a genuine positive feedback loop: preference and trait can drive each other past what viability selection alone would allow. Zahavi predicts the same outcome for a different reason, cost as the honest signal. Either logic, if it holds here, needs no external resource to sustain complexity; the mate choice itself becomes the sustaining force.

**A cheap, deterministic mechanism.** The hue based compatibility window already scores potential mates. Add a second, small term to that score, computed from a number the game already tracks, part count or organ presence, so a more complete body wins a marginally better chance within the existing forty degree hue window. No new physics, and if the term is a pure function of both genomes it needs no new draw from the random stream at all.

**How to measure it.** Compare assortative pairing by complexity, not only by hue, using the telemetry already built for hue assortative preference. Watch whether the crown run's golden age extends past its previous collapse point under the new scoring, and track rent the way brain weight was already tracked rising under predation.

**Risks.** The literature flags this lever as unstable by design: runaway is a documented outcome, not a failure mode, and with gene ranges pinned here, a population may simply run to the ceiling and stop. Small population size sharpens the risk: Lande's models tie runaway strength to genetic variance and population size, both modest in a dish that holds hundreds, not thousands, so a real signal may be hard to separate from the seed dependent noise that already made the meaning of calls a coin flip. This project's own history is the clearest determinism warning: nearly every broken replay traces back to an uninitialized field or an unlogged draw, exactly what a new stochastic scoring term risks unless built as a pure function from the start.

## Entanglement: developmental one way doors

**What the literature predicts.** Constructive neutral evolution predicts that dependency between parts can accumulate with no selection for the dependency itself, and that once several parts depend on each other, reversal becomes unlikely for combinatorial reasons alone. Generative entrenchment predicts the same from the development side: the earlier and more heavily a structure is built upon, the harder it becomes to remove cheaply, and major transitions theory shows the same lock in at the scale of whole organelles.

**A cheap, deterministic mechanism.** The unified principle already ties a ganglion to every part. Entanglement would let a part's effective shed cost, or the metabolic classification of its rent, scale with how long it has been fixed in the lineage and how many downstream weights or organs reference it: a pure function of stored lineage history, computed at the same growth ticks that already cache body state. Nothing about it requires a new draw from the random stream.

**How to measure it.** This is the one lever with data already sitting on disk. The Bolder Mutations follow up already found that only structure paired with an eye stopped shrinking, seeds 2727 and 60606, precisely what entrenchment predicts when checked after the fact: the eye did not just pay for itself, it became something other systems leaned on. A first pass can re read crown-and-replication.json and collision-ecology.json for that signature before any new mechanic is written.

**Risks.** Entanglement cannot ignite complexity on its own; it only changes how expensive complexity is to lose once present, so it does nothing about most worlds never reaching real depth at all. Too strong, it becomes the problem it is named for: a population defending a structure past the point it earns its keep, weakening the same reversibility that let seed 82828 wipe to zero parts and regrow. The determinism flag is the project's own: every past body or brain change touching per creature state has broken a replay once through an uninitialized field, so this risk is procedural, not conceptual.

## Ranked recommendation

Build entanglement first. The emergence half of this problem is already solved and the retention half is not: branching ignites everywhere, and the crown run's collapse, eyes holding fifty minutes then gone within eighteen, looks more like a ratchet giving way all at once than a slow cost benefit reversal, the shape constructive neutral evolution predicts for interdependent parts. It is also the cheapest lever to test, since its signature may already sit in existing study data, and it carries the lowest runaway and determinism risk, since it adds no new feedback loop, only a modifier on one that exists.

Necessity second. It reuses the reef precedent directly and is measurable with a protocol already run once, but that precedent is also the clearest reason for caution: the garden effect it most resembles did not replicate cleanly, so calibration will matter more than the mechanism itself.

The peacock ratchet third, not because the literature doubts it, Fisher, Lande, and Kirkpatrick are as solid as sexual selection theory gets, but because its predicted success mode is instability, and this project's populations are still small enough that a genuine runaway and a seed dependent fluke may be hard to tell apart, the same trap the speech experiment already fell into once.

**Where the literature says to combine them.** Necessity and entanglement pair naturally: a resource gate gives a structure its original reason to exist, and entanglement, in the language of Gould and Vrba's exaptation (1982), lets it outlive that reason once other systems depend on it. Zahavi's logic argues for adding the peacock ratchet later: a handicap is honest only if genuinely costly, and a structure already gating a real resource is a costlier, more honest ornament than an arbitrary one. The riskiest pairing is the peacock ratchet with entanglement before either is tested alone: an unbounded ornament that cannot be cheaply shed is a classic cautionary tale, and it deserves a release valve, some way to shed structure under real stress, before it ships.
