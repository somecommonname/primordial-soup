ROADMAP

A small evolving universe in one HTML file. No dependencies, no scripts telling creatures what to become, just genomes, physics, and selection.

NORTH STAR

A dish that keeps surprising us. Every feature must create selection pressure, never a scripted outcome. If we can predict exactly what evolves, the feature failed. The long goal: climb the ladder from parameter evolution (tuning knobs on a fixed animal) to open-ended evolution (new kinds of things keep appearing).

DESIGN PRINCIPLES

  1. Physics, not scripts. Mechanics are costs and opportunities; behavior emerges. Predators became viable through geometry fixes, not stat buffs.
  2. Measure before tuning. Every balance change came from instrumenting a single creature and watching it live or die. Napkin math lies; telemetry doesn't.
  3. Bugs are field notes. The best moments were diagnoses: creatures orbiting food they couldn't reach, hunters starving mid-contact, the rim carousel. Keep the stories.
  4. The dish must survive the player. Catastrophes can devastate but never brick the toy: extinction triggers spontaneous generation.
  5. One file. The whole universe stays readable in one sitting.

THE ROUTE AHEAD · three horizons

Rewritten 2026-09-11. The old NEXT and LATER lists were retired because seven of their ten items had already shipped: WebGL in v1.24, the boards and callsigns in v1.25, Amber alongside them, brains in v1.31, morphology across v1.11 to v1.36. What follows is what is actually left.

Horizon one · the persistence frontier

A world can make a complex animal but cannot keep one. Every world grows multi part bodies, and every world gives them back. The argument and the three candidate levers are in design/DESIGN-PERSISTENCE.md.

  • NOWNecessity, as the Garden: give the algae genomes, so the food supply coevolves and the world stops being solvable. Promoted by v1.40, and its case strengthened by v1.41: if eyes have never been selected for at all, the economy has never contained a reason to keep one.
  • NOWA heritable longevity gene: the breeder's line proved lifespan was never in the genome, so passaging could not select for it. Longevity against fertility or metabolism, measured like everything else.
  • NEXTThe peacock ratchet: heritable mate preference weighing display against rent. Third as ranked, because its success mode is instability and these populations are small.
  • NEXTEntanglement, recalibrated: shipped dormant in v1.40 after failing its prewritten test at a half life of twenty generations. A sweep would tell us whether that was the wrong mechanism or the wrong dial.
  • NEXTThe Paper, fourth edition: six studies since the third are written up nowhere but in studies/.

Horizon two · the ladder of individuality

Persistence is a floor; these are the rungs the Body Mountain did not reach, and each is a transition in what counts as an individual.

  • NEXTSymbiosis, made structural: section 3.6 already shows parasites walking down into mutualists. Let one creature live inside another and the largest event in the history of life becomes available in a petri dish.
  • LATERMulticellularity: offspring that do not detach, colonies, division of labour. The Body Mountain climbed individuality within one body; this climbs it above one.
  • LATERCulture that amplifies: calls, scent, and memory all exist and meaning stays a faint hum. Learning within a lifetime would give the dish a second inheritance channel, faster than genes.

Horizon three · what it becomes

  • LATERThe boards, live: built, security reviewed, and dark. BOARDS_URL is empty and the 🌐 button hides itself. Until it deploys, the project's loudest open question, what players find that test dishes did not, cannot be asked at all.
  • LATERThe menu-bar pet: a native always-on dish (Tauri) in the corner of the screen; true 24/7 evolution, glanceable like a fish tank.
  • LATERThe Classroom, taken seriously: six seeded experiments already ship, and every student gets a bit identical dish, which turns a class into real replicates.
  • LATERThe Paper, submitted: fifteen findings, prewritten criteria, falsifiers, honest limits. ALIFE or ECAL, or published on the web beside the dish.

Foundations · debts that block the horizons

  • DEBTCross machine determinism is untested, and v1.40 found the first crack: seed 60606 builds an identical world on another machine (same callsign, same founders) but diverges in play. The boards' anti cheat story rests on re running an entry from its seed.
  • DEBTThe board verifier: storage for seeds and logs is in place; the verifier is unwritten.
  • DEBTTwo open security findings from the 2026-08-17 review, in leaderboard/SECURITY.md. Neither should be open when a public endpoint goes up.
  • DEBTExact resume: saves quantize positions, so every reload nudges a live world off the path its own replay reconstructs. True since v0.5.
  • DEBTThe long runs nobody has done: whether reefs outlast freeloaders, and whether ordinary dishes walk parasites down into symbionts as a rule. The Shelf makes both practical.

SHIPPED

v0.1 · The dish

Genomes (size, speed, vision, diet, fear, fertility, wander), energy economics, algae blooms, god tools (meteor, plague, ice age, bloom, seed), dials for food and radiation, an inspector with named creatures, population and trait-drift charts.

Field notes: creatures orbited food they couldn't reach (turn radius exceeded eat radius) and starved amid plenty; fixed with arrival slowdown and opportunistic grazing. The first dish evolved couch potatoes, slowness as a survival strategy.

v0.2 · Predation that works

Target lock with a give-up timer, opportunistic biting, lead pursuit (intercept-point aiming; pure pursuit provably loses to a tighter-turning dodger), satiation, reproduction cooldown, diet-scaled metabolism, quadratic meat digestion so only specialists profit, nutrient recycling from corpses.

Field notes: the dish is too small for stable apex wolves (two or three sustainable individuals go extinct by chance), so the persistent niche is the jackal. A cannibal-soup collapse taught us casual meat-eating must not pay. The dish rim was accidentally a predator-proof sanctuary; fixing it turned the rim into a hunting ground.

v0.3 · Sex, species, herds

Sexual reproduction with courtship, uniform crossover plus mutation, hue-based mate compatibility (±40°) with assortative preference, a desperation-clone fallback for the lonely, a herd gene (kin cohesion, alignment, stampedes), and slowed color drift so speciation takes generations.

Field notes: sex became 93-98% of all births immediately. Herding verified (3× clustering) but selected against in predator-free dishes. Natives stay one species under panmixia: no isolation, no speciation, exactly as biology predicts; founder events via the seed tool create instantly isolated species. High radiation is a genuine sterilization ray.

v0.4 · Terrain

Rocks as physical obstacles (slide-along collision, prey get cornered, wanderers steer off) and current jets that wander across epochs, carry algae into visible food streams, and sweep small creatures while big ones resist; size is now also current-resistance. Marine snow traces the flow. Species counter, sexual/cloned birth counters, and divergence announcements in the HUD.

v0.5 · The dish lives on your machine

Persistent worlds: the dish auto-saves and resumes exactly where it left off (same rocks, same species, same history), and when you return it simulates the time you were away as a visible fast-forward ("⏩ while you slept: 214 born, 198 died"). Installable as an app (PWA), offline-capable. "New dish" asks before erasing a world, because worlds are worth something now.

Field notes: first step toward the ambient-companion goal. Verified by a save → wipe → restore → catch-up round-trip: the world returned atom-for-atom identical, then lived its missed five minutes to the exact second.

v0.6 · The tree of life

A live phylogeny panel. Every creature carries a lineage id; children inherit it until their color drifts past 30° from the lineage's founding hue, which founds a branch. Seeded strangers found new roots. Rendered as a streamgraph: band thickness is population, splits are speciation, fading bands are extinctions, and the envelope of the whole tree is the population curve. Adaptive downsampling keeps a long-lived dish's entire history in bounded memory, and the tree survives save/load, so it grows across visits and through catch-up time.

Field notes: a high-radiation test dish produced 81 lineages in eleven minutes, and the great famine appeared as a pinch in the stream. The tree records history; the species counter records current gene flow. They are different instruments.

v0.7 · Arms and gardens

Two builds in one. Armor and weapons: spike and shell genes with honest costs. Shells raise the size advantage a predator needs to crack you but slow you 28% and cost metabolism; quills wound attackers on contact, sometimes fatally. Predators account for shells when choosing targets. Plants that spread: algae now reproduce locally with logistic crowding instead of raining from the sky, leaving only a thin spore drizzle that strengthens over bare ground so deserts can recover. Corpses and meteor debris act as seeds, and the sunlight dial now scales growth.

Field notes: under seeded predation (157 kills), prey spikes nearly doubled and speed rose 22% in four minutes while shells rose only modestly. In a patchy-food world, defense that slows travel is too expensive, so evolution bought quills. Nobody designed that preference.

v1.0 · A finished game

Dish seeds: export any world as a compact compressed seed string and import someone else's; trade ecosystems, replay famous dishes, watch alien life take hold in your own dish. Sound: a quiet Web Audio voice for the dish (births blip, kills thud, meteors rumble, ice shimmers) with a mute toggle and a limiter so busy dishes stay gentle. A first-visit welcome card explains the game in four lines. Together with the tree of life, persistent worlds, and the living plant ecology, this closes the v1.0 milestone: a polished toy a stranger can enjoy with no explanation.

Field notes: a seed is the save format itself, so sharing a dish shares its whole fossil record: terrain, genomes, and family tree arrive intact.

v1.1 · Climate

The dish gets a warm end, a cold end, and a four minute year. Local temperature scales plant growth, metabolism, and movement speed: warm water is fast rich living, cold water is a slow larder. A twelfth gene, warmth, sets each creature's comfort zone with a discomfort cost for living outside it. Seasons swing the whole dish through spring, summer, autumn, and winter, shown in the report and as an amber and blue wash over the water. Also in this build: a real mobile layout with panels that fold to their headers, and link preview tags so shared links unfurl nicely.

Field notes: the warm side gets grazed bare while the cold side accumulates standing algae, tropics and poles in miniature; nobody designed that. An eighteen minute speciation watch found no sympatric split but strong local adaptation: the warmth gene tracked habitat from 0.6 down to 0.31, with the warm side subpopulation consistently warmer adapted. Adaptation is easy; speciation needs isolation.

v1.2 · Minds, plagues, and shadows

Three systems in one build. Neural brains, the frontier groundwork: every creature carries a 20 weight neural layer reading ten senses and outputting a steering nudge and thrust change, blended by a new brainW gene starting near zero. Neural tissue costs real energy, so brains must earn their influence through selection. Evolving parasites: a second replicator living on hosts, one virulence gene governing drain and spread, mutating at every transmission, countered by a new host immune gene with its own cost. Transmission by contact and by eating infected prey. Line of sight: rocks block perception; prey cannot fear what they cannot see, and ambush becomes real. Also: persistent player records that survive across dishes, with celebrations.

Field notes: the first epidemic saturated the dish in a minute thanks to a double scaled patient zero rate; cooled, outbreaks come in waves. Immunity drifted upward even without parasites until given a metabolic cost: nothing in the dish is free anymore.

v1.3 · Trophies and the first thought

The badge gallery: seventeen named achievements built on the records system, earned across dishes forever, opened by clicking the records line. The god tools reorganized into three blessings (bloom, seed, and the new elixir, which restores energy and cures parasites) and three catastrophes (meteor, plague, ice age), with inspect standing alone. A brain trait sparkline joins the report.

Field notes: the brain selection experiment delivered. Under seeded predation, average brain weight tripled from 0.05 to 0.148, then relaxed once the predators died out: evolution rented a brain when hunted and let the lease lapse in peacetime. Thought, in this dish, is a wartime expense.

v1.4 · Bloodlines and the Chronicle

Name any creature (the ✎ button in the inspector) and follow its line (the ☆ button): the report then tracks your bloodline forever, celebrating each ten generations and mourning the line's end if it comes. The dish also writes its own history now: the Chronicle records each year's births and deaths alongside meteors, plagues, ice ages, first infections, divergences, and badges, and it persists with the save. A Dynast badge honors any followed line that reaches generation 25.

Field notes: in the first test, a founder named Adam was followed, produced 85 living descendants by generation 13, and died of old age during his own experiment. The feature works: it already hurts.

v1.5 · Postcards and creature cards

The 📷 postcard button composes a shareable image of your dish: the living world, its stats, the tree of life, and the address. The 🎴 card button in the inspector renders any creature as a trading card: portrait drawn true to its genes plus all ten traits as bars. On phones both open the native share sheet; on desktop they download. A Photographer badge for your first postcard. Also: a visible version badge and self applying updates, so no device ever plays a stale build again.

v1.6 · Names for worlds, worlds to choose

Every dish now has a callsign, a unique identity like SOUP-K7F3-9Q, shown in the report, written into the Chronicle at birth, and stamped on postcards and creature cards with the date and season. New dish opens a world picker: Classic, Ice World, Eden, or Plaguelands. A 🪨 rock tool joins the panel: tap water to raise a rock, tap a rock to crumble it. An Iceborn badge honors keeping an Ice World alive to year 2.

v1.7 · Memory

The brain grows from a 20 weight reflex sheet into a 48 weight recurrent circuit with two memory cells: slow inner states with a roughly three second half life that the brain can write to and read from. All memory circuitry starts at zero: evolution must discover remembering on its own and pay for it. Old brains migrate losslessly. A Rememberer badge waits for the first population whose memory circuits carry real weight.

Field notes: the substrate verified in a controlled scare: a wired memory cell charged to half strength within a second of threat exposure and faded smoothly over seven seconds after the threat was removed. Fear that outlasts its cause is now physically possible; whether evolution buys it is the experiment that never stops running.

v1.8 · Signals

Creatures gain a voice and ears, and nothing else is given. A fifth brain output emits a visible pulse that costs real energy; three hearing inputs carry the direction and intensity of nearby calls. What a call means is defined nowhere in the code: meaning must evolve. The brain grows to 75 weights with all new circuitry at zero, old brains migrate losslessly, calls per minute joins the report, and a First Word badge waits for the first population that truly speaks.

Field notes: the channel verified end to end: a wired speaker emitted, the calls hung in the water, and a wired listener turned toward every one. The dish now waits for its first honest word, and for its first lie. Also in v1.8.1: a Field Guide panel teaching new players everything the dish can do, linked from the welcome card.

v1.9 · Pheromones

A scent field lives on the dish floor: a coarse grid costing six kilobytes, fading over about fifteen seconds. A sixth brain output deposits scent at real energy cost; three new senses smell the local intensity and gradient. Trails render as faint teal ghosts. Meaning is scripted nowhere: whether trails become roads to food, territorial markings, or lures is evolution's decision. The brain grows to 108 weights, every migration remains lossless, and a Trailblazer badge waits for the first people who leave trails worth following.

Field notes: a wired depositor painted its wandering across the floor and the field held its shape. Performance stayed light per David's rule: 0.6 milliseconds per step at a population of 117.

v1.10 · The Observatory

Watching evolution now feels like science. The 🔭 observe panel holds a live species census, dish vital signs, and the record holders, each one tap from visiting the creature itself. The tree of life answers questions: hover or tap any band to identify the lineage, its population, its birth year, and whether it has ended. The whole world exports as clean JSON: identity, records, badges, species statistics, trait histories, and the entire Chronicle. A Naturalist badge for the first export.

v1.11 · Morphology

Bodies leave the circle behind. Three shape genes with honest tradeoffs, all starting near zero so evolution sculpts its own anatomy. Elongation: a streamlined ellipse with cheaper straight line travel and a mouth at the nose, but slower turning. Fins: more thrust and current resistance at higher burn. Jaws: crack bigger and better shelled prey for a standing metabolic cost. Eels, sailfins, and crushers are now visibly different animals. A Shapeshifter badge honors the first population whose bodies truly leave the circle.

Field notes: max jaws lower the size ratio needed to crack a full shell from 1.79 to 1.36; armor stays formidable but no longer absolute. The genome then counted 17 genes plus 108 brain weights.

v1.12 · Lifecycles

Three new genes, three ways to spend a life. Eggs: lay richer provisioned offspring that incubate helpless for fourteen seconds; eggs are food, so the egg thief is now a possible profession. Care: guard your nest and provision your hatchlings, paying with slower rebreeding. Torpor: when cold and starving, sleep; metabolism crashes and so does your ability to flee. Winter, predators, and scarcity decide who becomes a devoted mother, an egg thief, or a hibernator. Clutch and Winter Sleepers badges; the genome reaches 20 genes.

Field notes: a tracked egg hatched on schedule with its provisioned energy intact, nest protection blocked raids while the parent stood watch, and torpor engaged exactly at the cold and hungry threshold.

v1.13 · Fungi and communion

The third kingdom rises, and the plague learns mercy. Death feeds fungi: corpses sprout violet decomposers that become rich scavenger food for anyone willing, or mature and burst into fresh algae, closing the loop from death back to life. And symbiosis: a parasite whose virulence mutates below 0.12 crosses into mutualism, granting a digestion bonus and blocking harmful strains from its host. Evolution can now walk a plague all the way down into a partnership. Mycelium and Communion badges; 29 in the gallery.

Field notes: death sprouted fungus, mature fungus burst into plants, and symbionts were carried, all verified while a natural epidemic raged through the test dish. This closes the science ladder that began with the first evolvable brain.

v1.14 · Destiny seeds

Every roll of fate flows through one seeded generator, so a dish is perfectly reproducible: the new dish picker accepts an optional seed, and the same seed builds the same world down to every rock, founder genome, and brain weight, with the same callsign. Verified beyond the requirement: two runs of the same seed matched even after ten simulated seconds of evolution. This is the foundation of the coming daily dish and dish versus dish. v1.15 · The daily dish and the war of two shores

The social tier arrives. 📅 Today's dish: one tap seeds the world from the date itself, so everyone who starts today starts the same world. ⚔ Versus: two seeds found two peoples on opposite shores of one arena, true separate species who cannot interbreed, and the dish referees: when one founding line's last descendant falls, the Chronicle records the victor. Day Player and Warbringer badges bring the gallery to 31.

Field notes: the versus referee was tested by exterminating the west twice, and twice the verdict correctly refused to come: the west survived once through eggs laid before the fall, and once because dying grazers ate their way back to life in their final frame. Only true extinction brought the verdict. A referee that cannot be rushed, and a lifecycle that defeats genocide by lunch and by egg, both working exactly as built.

v1.16 · The Time Machine

Every dish now logs each intervention, every meteor, bloom, elixir, rock, stranger, plague, ice age, and dial change, stamped with its exact simulation step. Guards keep history read only: saves, tools, and dials are locked while the machine runs. Old dishes predate their own records and say so honestly; every dish born from v1.16 onward carries its whole past. Field note: the first replays diverged from history because the sunlight dial applied its present value to the past; the fix logs the dials the dish was born with as step zero entries. A second bug: versus dishes forgot which two founding seeds built them; the pair is now part of the save. Verified bit identical replays across classic, versus, and non default dial dishes: same random stream, same positions, same energies. Historian badge (32).

v1.20 · Sound evolution

Every creature sings its genome. The root note comes from hue on a pentatonic scale, the octave from size (the big sing low), tempo from speed, intervals from diet (herbivores major, carnivores minor), timbre from armor (shells sing square waves, quills sawtooth), phrase length from brain power, vibrato from wander. Click any creature to hear its line; when a species diverges, the newborn lineage announces itself in song. Motifs are pure functions of the genome: no randomness, no effect on the simulation stream, so replays stay bit identical and evolution becomes audible as songs drift with the genes that carry them.

v1.23 · The Archipelago

One people, three lagoons. Sealed reef walls divide the dish into three seas joined only by narrow straits, and a single founding population is scattered across them. Geography does the rest: isolated lagoons drift apart in color and body until the strains counter climbs, which is allopatric speciation, the textbook mechanism the old panmictic dish could never produce. Migration is real and measured: rock sliding collision turns the reef walls into guide rails that funnel the bold through the passes, about a dozen crossings a minute in testing, each one counted with hysteresis so boundary loiterers do not inflate the ledger. The scent trails of wayfarers literally paint the migration highways on the reef. Wayfarer badge for witnessing ten crossings (38).

v1.24 · The ocean and the fast renderer

The 🌊 ocean scenario seeds a wide fertile sea, 120 founders and thick algae, and populations boom into the many hundreds. To draw them, a WebGL renderer now takes over above 250 cells: one buffer, one draw call, soft glowing oriented bodies with hue, elongation, energy, and torpor intact. Measured: 2,900 creatures render in 0.34 milliseconds where the canvas path needs 8.9, a 26x speedup with a flat cost curve, and the whole thing falls back to the hand drawn canvas automatically on small dishes and on browsers without WebGL. The toolbar also got its reckoning: nine panel launchers compressed into one three column grid, and the ticker moved to bottom center so updates never hide behind a growing toolbar again. And a confession from the audit: screen shake had been quietly drawing from the simulation's random stream every frame after a meteor, which made post meteor live timelines subtly unreplayable; shake now has its own private random stream, and the determinism promise is whole. Legion badge for a thousand cells alive at once (39).

v1.25 · Amber and the boards

The social layer begins. Amber is prestige that cannot be bought, only lived: a dish that survives two years earns its Fleck, five the Amber Seal (and a faint patina on the dish rim), ten Deep Amber, twenty Eternal Amber, honorific stars beside the callsign throughout. And the 🌐 boards: four world leaderboards, the daily dish, oldest world, deepest generation, longest dynasty, served by a one file Cloudflare Worker with a tiny database. There are no accounts and no names: a dish callsign is the whole identity, bound to a secret token on first publish so nobody can post as you. Cheating is structurally hard: simulated age is checked against the wall clock at maximum speed, the daily board only accepts today's shared world, and every entry carries its seed, so any suspicious world can in principle be re run and verified bit for bit, the time machine as anti cheat. Verified end to end against a local worker: publish, rank, rate limit honesty, dynasty entries, token theft rejection. The client ships dark until the worker deploys. Amberbound and Herald badges (41).

v1.26 · The Body Plan

The first rung of the open endedness ladder: genomes that grow. A new mutation can duplicate a body segment, literal gene duplication, and each segment carries its own fin, quill, and shell genes with real physics: 45 percent more energy storage per segment, more current resistance, body quills that punish biters and body shells that resist jaws, against 26 percent more metabolism, less speed, and a wider turning circle. Fins on segments claw thrust back. Every creature in every world starts bodiless, exactly as before, so bodies are not given, they are discovered: in the first test dish two creatures grew their first segment within two minutes under high radiation. Segments render as undulating trail following chains in both the canvas and WebGL paths, and worlds with evolved bodies replay bit identical. Field note: the first replay test failed until the test itself was found guilty; two probe creatures spawned between world birth and history consumed unlogged random draws, lab contamination, not a code bug. The sterile rerun matched exactly. Leviathan badge for a four segment body (42).

v1.27 · The Reef Builders

Niche construction: the world itself joins the genome's reach. A 21st gene, masonry, lets an energy rich creature pay six energy to lay living stone. Deposits near an existing reef grow it; fresh stone starts as a pebble. Reefs are real terrain, they block sight and movement like any rock, and they erode steadily without upkeep, so a reef is a living thing that dies with its keepers. The payoff is the garden: currents collect nutrients at reef edges, so algae sprout faster and pack denser there. Measured at grazed equilibrium in a hungry dish, the reef circle held six times the standing crop of a mirrored control; in times of plenty the effect vanishes into saturation. That is the experiment the dish now runs forever: gardens feed every grazer, mason or not, so building is a public good, and whether builders can outlast freeloaders is left to selection. Two bugs fell in passing: algae were never clamped to the dish rim, so currents had been quietly sweeping food off the edge of the world since terrain shipped, uneatable and still counted against the cap; and the garden effect was invisible in early tests because saturated dishes hide it, a lesson in measuring at the equilibrium that matters. Architect badge for forty stone of creature built reef (43). Patch v1.27.1: reef pebbles crashed the rock highlight arc with a negative radius and one draw error could freeze the whole loop; the arc is clamped, draw errors can no longer stop the world, and the site now keeps its own error log, surfacing problems in the ticker and storing the last eight on device.

v1.29 · Growing Up

The third rung of the ladder: bodies now develop. A newborn is small, roughly a third of its eventual adult size, and its inherited segments barely register; it grows smoothly into its full form by age twelve, the same boundary that already separated young from adult in the starvation statistics. Every physical consequence scales with growth: storage, metabolism, current resistance, combat reach and kill power, body armor and quills. A juvenile is genuinely smaller and softer prey than the adult it will become, and a juvenile predator has a genuinely smaller bite. Only adults breed now, on both sides of a courtship, so childhood is a real gauntlet and parental care earns its keep. Founders, seeded strangers, workshop releases, and heirs arrive already grown; only true births grow up. Growth is a pure curve with no random draws, so replays stay bit identical, and old saves need no migration since age was always stored. In passing, a determinism gap closed: creature id counters were never reset between worlds, so same seed dishes matched in every way except their ids; they now match down to the id. Populations run a little leaner under honest ontogeny, about a third fewer at equilibrium in testing, and thirteen generations in six minutes still replay exactly. Coming of Age badge for watching a creature born in your dish reach adulthood (44).

v1.30 · Diet chemistry

The last committed rung of the ladder: digestion becomes chemistry. The old single diet axis splits in two: appetite, the existing diet gene that decides what a creature tries to eat, and chemistry, two new enzyme genes that decide what its gut can actually extract. Algae, meat, fungus, and eggs each carry a chemical profile; plant efficiency runs on the plant enzyme, flesh on the meat enzyme with its old quadratic character, fungus rewards a mix, and a gut that runs both enzyme systems above a combined budget pays a standing metabolic tax, so specialists ride cheap and generalism must earn its overhead. The new evolutionary problem is coevolution: appetite and chemistry are inherited separately, so a lineage can want what it cannot digest until selection lines the two up. Old saves migrate seamlessly, every existing creature receives chemistry matched to its current appetite at near exact efficiency parity, verified to within a few percent at both extremes. Five minutes of the new economy holds steady, replays stay bit identical, and the workshop grew two sliders. Alchemist badge when three gut strategies, plant specialist, meat specialist, and paying generalist, share one dish (45).

v1.31 · The Second Thought

The first climb of the route agreed after the ladder: brains that grow. Until now every brain was a single layer, 108 weights mapping 18 senses straight to 6 actions, and a single layer has a hard mathematical ceiling: it cannot express nonlinear rules like flee when a predator is near or when starving, but not both. A structural mutation can now add a hidden neuron by duplication, the same trick that grows body segments, up to eight; each hidden neuron burns real energy through the existing wsum cost, so deeper thought must pay its way, the wartime brain economics extended to structure. Topology crosses over between parents of different depths, migrates losslessly from every older brain format (the new layout at zero hidden neurons is bit for bit the old one), and breeds true: in testing, thirty five founders gifted a hidden neuron all died of old age, yet three sim minutes later thirty nine of fifty two living creatures carried one, every single one of them born with it, paying about nineteen percent more brain upkeep and persisting anyway. Emergence in the wild is rare and slow, like segments before it, and that is the design: the first hidden neuron a dish evolves on its own writes itself into the chronicle. Verified bit identical replays at eighteen thousand steps, and a field note with a lesson: the first replay test failed until a single uninitialized field was found, a creature property that existed on some creatures and not others; the rule is now that no creature field is ever left undefined. v1.33 · Lighter bones

The first release of the Body Mountain route, and it changes no feature at all: it changes a price. The emergence studies showed body segments were never rare, only expensive: at twenty six percent extra metabolism per segment, only 6.5 percent of expected segment carriers survived a fifteen minute world. Three studies later, twenty two seeds and over twenty seven thousand births in total, the rent settled at ten percent: segment survival nearly tripled to 17.4 percent, populations came out healthier at every step of the reduction, and no world tipped into segment dominance. The honest caveat is preserved with the result: a single dish's outcome is dominated by a newly measured universal event, the founder bottleneck, in which every world loses roughly sixty percent of its founders between the first and second minute; who happens to carry structure through that crash shapes the rest of the run more than any constant we tune. The bottleneck itself is now on the research list. This is what measure then tune looks like when the measurement wins: the feature was already right, the price was wrong.

v1.34 · The First Branch

Chains are simply trees that never branched, so every existing creature migrated untouched, and the spine still swims with the trail following undulation while branches ride their parents geometrically with a gentle wobble. v1.35 · The Paddle

Phase two of the Body Mountain: shape becomes physics. Every fin part now beats with an analytic oscillation, no physics solver, just closed form: phase travels down the body by depth so spines undulate in a metachronal wave, thrust follows the cosine of a paddle's angle off the spine and torque follows the sine times its lever arm. The consequences write themselves: straight back fins are engines, angled fins are rudders, a one sided paddler veers three times harder than a mirrored one in testing, and bilateral symmetry stops being decoration and becomes engineering, which means the mirror mutation from v1.34 now pays for itself. The oscillation averages exactly one, so every existing chain keeps its mean speed and simply gains a lifelike surge; juveniles paddle weakly and grow into their stroke through the same growth curve as everything else. No new random draws, no save format change, sterile replays bit identical, 0.22 milliseconds per step. The chronicle now waits for the first wild side fin strong enough to row: the first paddle. v1.36 · The Organ

The final face of the Body Mountain. Studies · 2026-08-19 to 2026-08-23

Four studies ran after v1.39 without shipping a feature, and their raw data now lives in studies/ so it survives a scratchpad wipe. The incubator trials found that refugia beat population size, and that geography, not numbers, is what keeps complexity. The refugia geometry sweep found the shipped archipelago already near its optimum, and gave the project a law worth keeping: loose straits make worlds stable but sterile, and complexity needs a little chaos. v1.40 · The Entanglement

Old structure should be hard to shed. Every body part now carries two clocks: how many generations its slot has been inherited, and how long since its organs were last jolted. The shed rate decays with the first, organ drift is damped by the second, and Wimsatt's generative entrenchment gets a test. It failed its own prewritten criterion and ships dormant. Paired across eight seeds, lever off against lever on, complete animals stood at minute ninety in one seed of eight either way, where the bar was four. What the lever did do is real: parts were held twice as long, depth of at least two parts survived in seven seeds of eight against four, bodied share across the run rose from 32 to 45 percent, and populations came out slightly healthier rather than sicker, so this is not a museum. But any eyed creature at all survived in exactly two seeds of eight in both arms. The binding constraint was never structural retention; it is that nothing pays a lineage to keep an eye. One seed answered the question the study was named for: an entrenched world reached minute 84 with 31 of 51 creatures bodied and its oldest part 75 generations deep, then arrived at minute 90 with five bodied and its oldest part two. Long held structure does not erode, it gives way all at once. Entrenchment slows that ratchet without fitting it a pawl. The instrumentation stays because it produced the finding; the lever sits at ENTRENCH=0, which is bit for bit v1.39.2. Full data in studies/entanglement.json. A second finding arrived unbidden: this machine could not reproduce the archived crown run for seed 60606, building an identical world that then played differently, which is the first evidence that determinism does not travel between machines.

v1.41 · The Crowd

The dish was the browser window. R came from min(W,H)/2-26 and nothing else, so there was no arena parameter at all, and the ocean scenario was never a bigger world: it seeded 120 founders instead of 70 and dumped 4200 algae into a cap of 3000, in an arena identical to classic. WORLD is now a radius multiplier and every density scales with its square, across R, the scent grid, the canvas transform, the WebGL uniform, pointer hit testing, the caps, founders, and initial algae. WORLD=1 is identity, verified bit for bit on seed 60606.

That alone was not enough. Quadrupling the area moved equilibrium population only from 32 to 43, because the standing algae crop sits near ten in a classic dish, grazed to bare rock, so proportional regrowth contributes almost nothing and the food supply is dominated by the spore drizzle, which was an absolute six spores a second for the whole dish rather than a density. A world four times the area received exactly the same food. Scaled, the dish finally breathes: 32 creatures at WORLD 1, 110 at 2, 258 at 3, and 583 at WORLD 4, never below 544 across the back half of a run, at 1.386ms per step, just inside the 1.5ms budget.

Field notes: the experiment this was built for refuted its own hypothesis, in the more interesting direction. An eye costs four percent metabolic burn and the drift barrier at a population of 25 is about four percent, so the guess was that selection had never been able to see an eye at all. Raising the population fifteenfold did not rescue the eye. It suppressed it, from peak eyed shares of 5.9 and 44.4 percent in the two small worlds to 1.0 and 2.2 percent in the two large ones. The variance settles the reading: bodied share wandered with a standard deviation of 33.0 and 18.1 points in the small worlds and 2.8 and 7.6 in the large ones. Small populations swing between 7 and 98 percent bodied; large ones sit steadily near 15. Drift was never what prevented eyes. Drift is what produced them. A trait that reaches 44 percent of a population of eighteen and 2 percent of a population of five hundred is not being selected for, it is floating, and the celebrated golden ages all happened at populations near thirty. Two seeds, so this is a pilot and is reported as one, with the honest confound that a wider world changes travel and mixing alongside population. Full data and the cost report in studies/crowd.json.

The drift audit · 2026-09-12

No feature, a measurement. Six seeds classic against six archipelago at WORLD 3, lever off, matched on generations rather than minutes: every run stopped at four hundred generations, none on the clock and none extinct, taking about 315 simulated minutes each. Two answers came back.

The complete animal does not survive. Zero of twelve runs ended with a body two parts deep, a quarter of the population eyed, and a paddler present. At a population near thirty the same criterion was met in one seed of eight. Raising the population and running three times as long did not make complete animals rarer by chance; it made them absent. Peak eyed share averaged 2.3 percent in both arms, against the 44 percent a population of eighteen reached in the v1.41 pilot, and where eyes survived at all it was one to three individuals out of well over a hundred. The variance tells the same story from a third population size: bodied share now wanders with a standard deviation of 4.4 points in classic worlds and 5.7 in the archipelago, against 33.0 and 18.1 at population thirty. The bigger the population, the narrower and lower the outcome, which is what drift being squeezed out looks like.

The archipelago's advantage is untested, not disproved, and the reason is a mistake worth recording. archTerrain is written entirely in fractions of R, so scaling the world scaled the lagoons and the straits with it while creatures stayed the same size. The archipelago at WORLD 3 is a proportionally identical map whose channels are three times wider relative to a body, and it ran at about 38 strait crossings a minute against the dozen a minute the refugia geometry sweep measured, roughly 30 crossings per generation against seven. The sweep's own law says loose straits make worlds stable but sterile, and that is a fair description of what this arm did. The two arms came out close on everything, bodied share 13.4 against 15.3, peak eyed 2.3 against 2.3, population 174 against 150, but the comparison cannot separate refugia failing at high population from refugia never having been tested. The proper experiment holds lagoon size and strait width absolute and tiles more lagoons across the bigger world, keeping local structure while raising global numbers. Full data, with the geometry guard that stopped a spoiled first attempt, in studies/drift-audit.json.

v1.42 · The Ledger

An instrument, not a feature. Every creature now carries ateA and ateM, the energy it has taken from algae and from flesh across its life, and a fitness ledger records each creature once, at death, with its lifetime offspring, its lifespan, and what it ate, classified by the body it was born with. The ledger is off unless a study switches it on, and with it off the dish is bit for bit v1.41.1 on seed 60606. It is what finally made selection visible, and it answered two questions in one run across six seeds, four hundred generations each, and 396,109 recorded individuals.

The eye is worth nothing. At one body part, with 1,080 eyed individuals against 54,309 without, an eye changes lifetime reproductive success by −2.4 percent with a confidence interval of ±6.3. At two parts, −2.9 ±22.3. Neutral, with a hint of a cost and no hint of a benefit. The reason is in the code rather than the ecology: at line 1251 algae are found with plain sense and no eye term, while line 1274 extends prey detection by half again. The eye's entire benefit flows through the prey channel, and flesh is 0.23 percent of lifetime energy. Predation is not rare, 8.5 percent of deaths are kills and appetite sits at 0.76 carnivore, but it feeds almost nobody. Vision here is an organ for reading a channel that carries no calories. The hypothesis this study was built on, that the world was too uniform to be worth looking at, is refuted: algae cluster at a variance to mean ratio of 3.89 against 1.0 for a random field. The information was always there; the eye was never wired to it.

And the finding that dwarfs it: a single body part costs 40.3 percent of lifetime reproductive success, ±0.6. Bodiless creatures average 1.476 offspring, one part 0.881, two parts 0.536, three or more 0.498. At 130 standard errors this is the least ambiguous measurement the project has ever made. The designed cost is 10 percent metabolism per part; the realised cost is four times that, because parts do not only burn more, they eat less, 1,718 energy a lifetime against 2,220, since a heavier, slower-turning creature forages worse. v1.33 lowered segment rent from 26 to 10 percent using measurements taken near population thirty, where a 40 percent coefficient sat invisible beneath a 4 percent drift barrier. Three studies now converge on one root cause: this project tuned its economy in a regime where it could not see its own selection. Full data, and a preregistered prediction for wiring the eye to the food channel, in studies/eye-selection.json.

v1.43 · The Threshold

Two switches and a preregistered study, and the headline is a failure written down before it happened. THRESH_BASE measures breeding readiness against a creature's base storage rather than storage inflated 45 percent per body part; EYE_FOOD lets an eye extend algae detection exactly as it already extended prey detection. Both ship off, verified bit for bit against v1.42. The design, the six seeds, and both tests were committed in studies/threshold-prereg.md before any run.

The eye, wired to food, is selected against. Among one part creatures in the preregistered arm an eye changes lifetime reproductive success by −11.7 percent, 95 percent interval −18.1 to −5.3, against a bar of +10 percent and above zero. The falsifier written before the run applies: the wiring was not the problem, and the vision line is set aside. Where bodies are cheaper to breed the same eye turns mildly positive, +4.6 percent with an interval of +1.3 to +7.9, still short of the bar, and at two parts the signs flip the other way in both arms. Effects that small, that context bound, and that inconsistent point at a design limit as much as a law: eyed and eyeless creatures are not randomised, so an eye cannot be separated from the lineage that happens to carry it. Eyed share among bodied creatures never approached half in any seed, averaging 1.6 and 4.5 percent.

The breeding threshold is a real share of the body cost, about a third of it. Across six seeds a one part body costs 40.3 percent of reproductive success with the switch off and 26.1 percent with it on, so storage raising the bar for breeding explains 35 percent of the penalty; the two seed pilot had put it at 43. Bodied share of the living population rises from 14.6 to 21.1 percent.

Field notes: the study hit four faults in its own tooling, recorded because the next study inherits them. The game's resize handler regenerates terrain from the random stream, and a first attempt to block it failed its own test; the desktop app clears emulated viewports when a turn ends, which moved the dish rim in three tabs and voided three runs before a geometry guard stopped them; the new harness checked its stop every 60 steps where the last checked every 3,600, so the control arm came in 425 deaths short of v1.42 across six seeds, every bin at or below; and zsh does not word split an unquoted variable, which left the progress monitor reading nothing. The reproducibility check as preregistered therefore failed, and then passed exactly: with the stop rule aligned, seed 60606 reproduced v1.42's ledger in every field over 1.12 million steps, which also shows the v1.42 eye study was never touched by the resize hazard. Full data in studies/threshold.json.

v1.44 · The Burden

Two switches, a permanent study lab, and a randomised experiment. SEG_MOVE stops a body part slowing its bearer: no turning penalty, no division of paddle thrust, no damping of the current's push, while fins keep their thrust. SEG_BURN stops a part adding 10 percent to metabolism, while mouths and eyes keep their costs. Both ship off, verified bit for bit against v1.43. studies/lab.js turns the harness behind four studies into a committed tool that refuses to run until a preflight passes, and adds a common garden: beside a random living resident it releases three newborns that copy its genome, brain and lineage, two bodiless and one given a single bare part, so a trait can be measured apart from the lineage that carries it. The design, the analysis script and four predictions were committed in studies/burden-prereg.md before any run: THRESH_BASE × SEG_MOVE × SEG_BURN, eight arms, six seeds, 400 generations, ledger and garden, 97 runs.

The body cost is caused by the part, and it is almost all movement. Given to one of three otherwise identical newborns, a bare part cuts lifetime offspring by 52 percent (49 to 56), against the ledger's 40 for parts that evolved. Freeing movement alone takes the cost to 2 percent in the ledger and 6 in the garden, both intervals including zero. Divided fairly among the three switches (Shapley shares of the default cost), movement takes 0.95 in the ledger and 1.01 in the garden, the breeding threshold 0.49 and 0.45, metabolism 0.44 and 0.57. The garden shows the mechanism (exploratory): a bare part founder eats 30 percent less algae than its identical siblings, since a finless part divides paddle thrust by 1.2, and with movement freed it eats 8 percent more. Two of four predictions failed, both about movement: I expected metabolism to dominate and movement to stay under a seventh of the cost, reasoning that a slower body also burns less.

Remove the penalties and a part becomes an advantage, and bodies take over. The shares sum to about two because the costs overlap: remove any two and a part costs nothing or pays, remove all three and it is worth 35 percent more offspring in the ledger and 54 in the garden. What a part still does then is store 45 percent more energy, and in a dish where most creatures die starving a bigger store is worth that much. Bodied share of the living population goes from 14 percent to 70 with movement freed and to 99 with all three off, averaging 5.5 parts against a cap of six. That is selection, not drift, favouring more body structure, at a mean population of 423. It is not yet the target: what selection favours there is storage, a side effect of how capacity is computed, not anything a part does with its organs.

Field notes: the lab passed every check it was built to make. Every tab's preflight passed; the acceptance test reproduced v1.42's seed 60606 ledger in every field; all twelve runs with the new switches off reproduced v1.43 field for field; and the garden's built-in null, two identical controls compared with each other, came out at −1.2 percent (−3.6 to +1.2) across 48 runs, though one arm of eight excluded zero on its own. The Browser pane closed seconds into the first launch and took all eight tabs with it before any run finished, so the tabs were relaunched one at a time. Worlds with penalties removed grew bigger populations of many-part creatures and ran up to 2.7 times slower; the study took 176 minutes across eight tabs. Full data in studies/burden.json, every run in studies/burden-runs.json.gz.
